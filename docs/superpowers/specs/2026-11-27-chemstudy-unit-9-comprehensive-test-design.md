## 4. What each part asks

As Unit 1's and Unit 6's tests taught, the task table was made after an
avoid list that indexes the pages' text as well as their questions, and
every task was compared with the pages before any writer starts
(`scratchpad/unit9-test/avoid-list.md` and `task-table.md`; twenty-two
first drafts were dropped in that comparison). The task table carries each
task in full, with its reactions, values and worked numbers. In short:

**Part A: Entropy (9.1, 9.2).** A1: four flasks of krypton, ranked where
the data allow and not where they do not. A2: a made-up table of kinetic
energies in bands, read for the hotter sample and the higher entropy, with
one column that cannot be right. A3: a sour-gas flare, and the S° of sulfur
dioxide found from ΔS°. A4: for three reactions, which ΔS° can be
calculated from the table, and which signs are certain without it. A5:
bromine, methanol and mercury vaporizing, and a claim tested.

**Part B: Gibbs free energy and favorability (9.3).** B1: urea from ammonia
and carbon dioxide: two values of ΔG° read back to ΔS°, ΔH° and the
temperature where the sign changes. B2: which metal oxides hydrogen
reduces, a rule read from ΔG°f values and tested on tin(IV) oxide. B3:
signs of ΔH° and ΔS° deduced from where four made-up reactions are favored.
B4: baking soda in an oven, the whole chain from two tables. B5: carbon
monoxide and soot: one reaction as written, halved and reversed.

**Part C: Kinetic control (9.4), free energy and equilibrium (9.5).** C1: a
sulfuric acid plant's converter: K and the time to reach equilibrium at
four temperatures. C2: light on hydrogen with chlorine and on silver
chloride in a lens. C3: a table of ΔG° and K with two pairs that cannot be
right. C4: carbon with steam: K against temperature, ΔG° by two routes, and
"none forms" refuted with a pressure. C5: two sugar phosphates: a mixture
read back to ΔG° and set against RT.

**Part D: Dissolving (9.6), coupled reactions (9.7).** D1: sodium nitrate,
silver chloride and calcium hydroxide: three sign patterns, a claim tested,
and a K. D2: magnesium sulfate and Epsom salt. D3: one favored reaction
asked to pay for one ATP and for two. D4: milk of magnesia and acid as a
coupled pair.

**Part E: Galvanic and electrolytic cells (9.8).** E1: three reports of
mass changes for a chromium and silver cell. E2: the salt for the bridge of
a cadmium and lead cell, and the ions counted. E3: copper refined in one
tank. E4: a block of zinc on a steel hull.

**Part F: Cell potential and free energy (9.9).** F1: three unknown metals
measured against cobalt. F2: magnesium, aluminum and zinc against tin,
ranked on four bases. F3: copper(I) ions reacting with themselves, and two
half-reactions added through ΔG°. F4: tin in lead nitrate: a mixture at
equilibrium read back to E°. F5: nitric acid parting silver from gold.

**Part G: Cells away from standard conditions (9.10).** G1: five readings
of a lead and copper cell, one of which cannot be right. G2: an iron and
cadmium cell that runs either way, decided by Q against K. G3: a manganese
and acid cell with a strong and a weak acid. G4: three beakers of tin(II)
chloride ordered by what their concentration cells do.

**Part H: Electrolysis and Faraday's law (9.11).** H1: three gold-plating
runs, one logged wrongly. H2: silver, palladium or cadmium, from a mass and
a charge. H3: oxygen for a crew from water. H4: a zinc and cadmium cell
until the cadmium ions run out. H5: manganese, with part of the charge making
hydrogen.

Sixteen tasks sit near lesson text or a source's scene and are marked for
the originality check: A3, B2, B4, C1, C2, C4, C5, D1, D2, D3, D4, E1, E2,
F3, F5 and G1.

## 6. Keeping the questions original, and the values honest

- Every question is written fresh from the framework's learning objectives,
  with its own substances, numbers and setting.
- **The writers read no textbook and none of the old course.** They read
  the framework summary, the site's Unit 9 lessons and staged practice, the
  avoid list, the task table and their part's pool.
- **One pool per part** (`scratchpad/unit9-test/pools.md`). No two parts
  share a scene or a reaction. A half-cell or a table value may serve two
  parts; the cell built from it may not repeat, and none of the 26 cells
  the lessons, the practice and the teacher's materials already set is
  built again (the avoid list names them).
- **An independent originality check**, one per session, compares every new
  question with the teacher's notes, review sheet, map, self-study, unit
  free-response set and worksheets, and the chapter notes on entropy, free
  energy and electrochemistry (each file named one by one; never an `exam`
  folder, never a file with "exam" or "test" in its name), and with the
  site's own lessons and practice. No textbook can be read from this
  session.
- **Values are honest, and none could be looked up.** So:
  - a stem gives every value its question uses ("use these values", or
    "take" for a value that is less sure), as the lessons say an exam
    question does;
  - **a question draws only on values in the Unit 9 lessons' table**
    (lessons design, section 3, and `scratchpad/unit9/lesson-table.md`),
    **or on a new value listed below with how sure its author was**;
  - the number check recomputes every answer from the stem's own values;
  - each fact-checker judges every value against what it knows; a value
    two checkers doubt is replaced or the question says "take";
  - every value the checkers could not confirm is listed for the teacher.

## 7. How each session is made

1. **The avoid list, the task table and the pools** (done).
2. **Writers, in parallel**, one per part, on the strongest model, files
   only (`writer-brief.md`).
3. **A number check.** Each writer leaves a script that recomputes every
   number in its part from the question's own data and asserts each against
   the page; the controller runs every script.
4. **The parts' substances, cells and values compared** across both
   sessions.
5. **An independent fact-check** per part, on the strongest model, by a
   fresh agent that solves every question before reading its answer, scores
   its own answer with the rubric, and applies each rule the page states to
   every example on it (`factcheck-brief.md`).
6. **Fixes**, by a fixer on Sonnet that applies a list the controller has
   decided and decides nothing itself (`fixer-brief.md`). A correction to a
   number is recomputed by the controller first.
7. **The originality check**, one agent per session, on Sonnet
   (`originality-brief.md`), and **the rewrites it asks for**, by a reviser
   per part on the strongest model (`reviser-brief.md`).
8. **A second check** on the strongest model, of two parts at a time, told
   to treat every sentence as new; last fixes.
9. **Assembly** (`assemble.py`), a build, the unit and browser tests, and
   each page measured at 412, 390, 360 and 320 px with every answer open,
   after every round of fixes.
10. **A pull request**, merged when its check passes on GitHub.

**Layout and markup, as learned since Unit 6** (the writer's brief carries
each in full): about 25 rendered characters to a display line at 320 px;
`aligned` with `&` on every row; inline math cannot wrap, so an equation in
a rubric line or an option is in words or a display; three short columns at
most in a table inside a part; a state symbol on every species; every
quantity with its unit; J/K turned to kJ/K on its own line before any
subtraction; joules shown before kilojoules in ΔG° = −RT ln K and
ΔG° = −nFE°; a potential never multiplied by a coefficient; a comma in
numbers of a thousand or more; charge written q; each rubric line on one
line, agreeing with its part's answer, the lines summing to the question's
points.
