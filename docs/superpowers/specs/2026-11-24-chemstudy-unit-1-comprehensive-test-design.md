# ChemStudy: a Unit 1 comprehensive test

Written on 2026-11-24. It follows the comprehensive tests of Units 5, 2, 7
and 3 (the designs of 2026-11-13, 2026-11-14 and 2026-11-15) and changes
nothing in them, or in the main design.

## 1. Why

Unit 1 has its eight lessons and its six practice documents, waiting in pull
requests. The teacher asked for the unit "end to end fully" (2026-11-16), and
the other finished units each end with a comprehensive test. The teacher's
old Unit 1 material has no comprehensive test to convert, and its unit exam
stays closed. **Nothing here is merged without the teacher's word.**

Counts and points are targets; a part may end a question or a few points
either way if that makes better questions.

**Spectra are drawn where reading the drawing is the question.** Parts B and
F may each have up to two drawn spectra, in the style of the practice's two
figures (pull request #60 gave practice pages the figure block). Each is

a table of its values beneath it. Every other spectrum is a table. Particle
pictures are described in words or as counts, and a question may ask for a
sketch on paper, answered in words.

## 4. What each part asks

still free). The standard kinds are used up: an average atomic mass from a
table has been set nine times on the site, "write the configuration" about

turned around.

## 5. Beyond the exam

The framework leaves out three things in Unit 1: mass spectra of samples
with several elements, and peaks from anything but singly charged single
atoms (1.2); quantum numbers (1.5); the configurations of elements that are
exceptions to the filling order (1.7). No question asks for any of them: no
molecular peaks, no chromium or copper atom, no ion whose configuration rests
on such an exception. Lattice energy and the properties of ionic solids are
Unit 2's and are not asked; separating a mixture is Unit 3's and is not
asked; combustion analysis, which the framework does not name, is not asked.
Electron affinity is not asked: lesson 1.7 gives it as a table and says not
to lean on it. If a question needs anything else the framework does not ask
for, that part is labeled "Beyond the exam".

## 6. Keeping the questions original, and the values honest

- Every question is written fresh from the framework's learning objectives,
  with its own substances, numbers and setting.
- **The writers read no textbook and none of the old course.** They read the
  framework notes, the site's Unit 1 lessons and practice, the avoid list
  and their part's pool.
- **One pool per part.** From what the avoid list leaves free, each part is
  given its own elements, isotope sets and compounds, so that two parts do
  not pick the same one. The ten things used most across the sources (the
  isotopes of chlorine, copper and boron; silicon; magnesium; aluminum; the
  phosphorus and sulfur break; calcium; strontium; water, carbon dioxide,
  sodium chloride and glucose) are used nowhere. The controller compares the
  parts before the fact-checks.
- **Settings.** The avoid list names every real-world setting the site
  already uses for a Unit 1 idea; none is used again.
- **An independent originality check**, one per session, then compares every
  new question with both textbooks, the teacher's notes and practice (never
  the exam), and the site's own lessons and practice. A question that repeats
  a scenario, a substance with its numbers, or one of the site's own kinds of
  question with a new element, is rewritten. A shared substance alone is not
  a finding.
- **Values are honest.**
  - Atomic masses are the exam table's (`src/data/elements.ts`), and every
    molar mass is their sum to two decimal places.
  - Isotope masses and abundances are measured values from a reference table
    (NIST), named in the part's number check. A question may instead use an
    element it calls made up ("element X"), with values that are consistent
    with one another.
  - First ionization energies, radii and electronegativities are the site's
    (`src/lib/periodic-trends.ts`); binding energies are the site's
    (`src/lib/pes.ts`, hydrogen to calcium). No photoelectron spectrum of an
    ion is given with values, since the site has none: an ion's spectrum is
    asked about in words.
  - Successive ionization energies are reference values (NIST), named in the
    number check.
  - A ranking a question asks for is true by the data.
  - Avogadro's number is 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.
- **What the practice's fact-checks kept finding**, so that the test does not
  repeat it: an answer with no reason, or with a reason that says more than
  is true (the tallest peak deciding an average of five peaks; a trend with
  no "generally"); a rule that fails on one of the page's own examples; a
  scoring note that a grader could read two ways; a correction to a number
  that was not recomputed.

## 7. How each session is made

1. **The avoid list** (done) **and the pools**, once, before session 1.
2. **Writers, in parallel**, one per part, files only.
3. **A number check.** Each writer leaves a script that recomputes every
   number in its part from the question's own data and asserts each against
   the page; the controller runs every script.
4. **The figures**, drawn from the writers' tables, verified by recovering
   every peak from the file.
5. **The parts' substances compared across the session.**
6. **An independent fact-check** per part, by a fresh agent that solves
   every question before reading its answer, scores its own answer with the
   rubric, applies each rule the page states to every example on it, and
   confirms every reference value against a second source.
7. **The originality check**, one agent per session.
8. **Fixes** (any correction to a number recomputed by the controller), **a
   second check**, a build, the unit and browser tests, and each page
   measured at 412, 390, 360 and 320 px with every answer open, after every
   round of fixes.
9. **A pull request**, left for the teacher, its check watched on GitHub.

**Models.** Writers and fact-checkers on Fable 5.2; the originality checks
and the fixers on Sonnet.
