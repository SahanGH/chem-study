## Questions

<Question number={1} title="Two nitrogen oxide ions" topic="2.5, 2.6, 2.7" points={10}>

Answer all parts. Nitrogen and oxygen form several species. This question examines $\ce{NO2-}$ and $\ce{NO2+}$.

<Part label="a">

Give the total valence electron count for each species.

<Answer>

$\ce{NO2-}$:

$$
5 + 2 \times 6 + 1 = 18
$$

$\ce{NO2+}$:

$$
5 + 2 \times 6 - 1 = 16
$$

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="d">

Predict the O–N–O bond angle in each species and justify the difference.

<Answer>

$\ce{NO2+}$: 180°. There are 2 domains, so it is linear.

$\ce{NO2-}$: less than 120° (about 115°). There are 3 domains, one of them a lone pair on N. The lone pair repels more strongly than the bonding domains and pushes the O–N–O angle below 120°. The ion is bent.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="e">

In $\ce{NO2-}$, the two N–O bonds are equal in length. Explain using your diagrams from (c).

<Answer>

The true structure is the resonance hybrid, an average of the two structures, so each bond has order $3/2$ and identical length.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="f">

Predict which species has the shorter N–O bonds, and justify with bond order.

<Answer>

$\ce{NO2+}$. Its bond order is 2, against $3/2$ in $\ce{NO2-}$. Higher order, shorter bond.

</Answer>

</Part>
