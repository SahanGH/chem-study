<Part label="b">

$\ce{H2S}$: domains, shape, angle, and whether it is polar.

<Answer>

Domains: 4 (2 lone pairs). Shape: bent. Angle: less than 109.5°: the two lone pairs push the bonds closer together. Polar: yes. The shape is bent, with both lone pairs on one side, so nothing cancels; sulfur also pulls slightly harder than hydrogen.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="d">

$\ce{PCl3}$: domains, shape, angle, and whether it is polar.

<Answer>

Domains: 4 (1 lone pair). Shape: trigonal pyramidal. Angle: a little less than 109.5°: the lone pair pushes the bonds closer together. Polar: yes.

</Answer>

<Question number={2} topic="2.7">

$\ce{CH4}$, $\ce{NH3}$ and $\ce{H2O}$ all have four electron domains, yet their bond angles run 109.5°, 107° and 104.5°. Write the three-part answer that explains a bond angle: count the domains, distinguish the molecules by what differs, and conclude with the angle and the reason.

<Answer>

All three have 4 domains in a tetrahedral arrangement. They differ in lone pairs: 0, 1 and 2. Lone pairs repel more strongly than bonding pairs, so each added lone pair compresses the angle further: from 109.5° in $\ce{CH4}$ to about 107° in $\ce{NH3}$ and about 104.5° in $\ce{H2O}$.

</Answer>

</Question>

<Question number={5} topic="2.7">

A molecule $\ce{AB3}$ is measured to be nonpolar with 120° angles. Another $\ce{AB3}$ molecule is polar with angles near 107°. Deduce the electron-domain picture of each central atom.

<Answer>

Nonpolar, 120°: 3 domains, no lone pairs (trigonal planar, symmetric). Only 3 domains give 120°.

Polar, 107°: 4 domains with 1 lone pair (trigonal pyramidal). 107° is 109.5° squeezed, so there are 4 domains; with 3 B atoms, the fourth is a lone pair. The lone pair breaks the symmetry.

</Answer>

</Question>
