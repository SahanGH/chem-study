Work each question on paper before you open its answer. One idea runs through the separation questions: every separation uses one physical difference between the substances.

For the solubility questions, name the structural feature, such as a polar bond, an O–H or a long carbon chain. The word "polar" alone is not a reason.

## Questions

<Question number={1} topic="3.10">

Predict which member of each pair is more soluble in water, and give the structural reason.

<Part label="a">

$\ce{CH3CH2OH}$ or $\ce{CH3CH2CH2CH2CH2OH}$

<Answer>

Ethanol. Both have one O–H group, which hydrogen-bonds with water, but the longer hydrocarbon chain of pentanol can offer water only weak attractions, so a smaller part of the molecule can replace the hydrogen bonds water gives up.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

$\ce{I2}$ or $\ce{NaI}$

<Answer>

$\ce{NaI}$. Its ions are stabilized by strong ion–dipole attractions with water; $\ce{I2}$ is nonpolar, so it can attract water only weakly, by London dispersion and dipole–induced dipole forces, far less than the hydrogen bonds water would give up.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

$\ce{CH4}$ or $\ce{NH3}$

<Answer>

$\ce{NH3}$. It hydrogen-bonds with water; $\ce{CH4}$ is nonpolar, so it can attract water only weakly, by London dispersion and dipole–induced dipole forces.

</Answer>

</Part>

</Question>

<Question number={2} topic="3.10">

Vitamin A is composed almost entirely of carbon and hydrogen. Vitamin C contains several O–H and C–O bonds.

<Part label="a">

Which is water-soluble? Justify from structure.

<Answer>

Vitamin C. Its polar O–H groups hydrogen-bond with water (hydrophilic); vitamin A is essentially nonpolar (hydrophobic).

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

Explain why excess vitamin A can accumulate in the body while excess vitamin C does not.

<Answer>

Vitamin A dissolves in the body's fats and is stored, mainly in the liver. Excess vitamin C stays dissolved in the watery blood and is excreted in urine.

</Answer>

</Part>

</Question>

<Question number={3} topic="3.10">

Iodine is far more soluble in $\ce{CCl4}$ than in water, even though water is the more common solvent. Explain using "like dissolves like."

<Answer>

$\ce{I2}$ and $\ce{CCl4}$ are both nonpolar, so the London dispersion forces between them are comparable to those being replaced. Dissolving $\ce{I2}$ in water would require breaking strong hydrogen bonds between water molecules and replacing them with much weaker $\ce{I2}$–water attractions, which is not favorable.

</Answer>

</Question>

<Question number={5} topic="3.9">

A paper chromatogram is run with a polar stationary phase (paper) and a nonpolar mobile phase. Two dyes are applied. Dye X travels 7.2 cm and dye Y travels 2.1 cm, while the solvent front travels 9.0 cm.

<Part label="a">

Which dye is more polar? Justify.

<Answer>

Dye Y. It moved less, meaning it was held more strongly by the polar paper (stationary phase).

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

Compute both retention factors ($R_f$ = distance moved by substance ÷ distance moved by solvent).

<Answer>

X: 0.80. Y: 0.23.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

Predict what happens to dye Y's $R_f$ if a polar mobile phase is used instead.

<Answer>

It increases. A polar solvent attracts the polar dye more strongly, competing with the paper for it, so it carries the dye farther up the paper.

</Answer>

</Part>

</Question>

<Question number={6} topic="3.9">

A mixture contains sand, salt, and water. Design a full separation, giving the order and the reason for each step.

<Answer>

Step 1: filter, which removes sand by particle size.

Step 2: distill or evaporate the filtrate, which separates water from salt by volatility: water evaporates and the salt does not. Collect the condensed water if it is wanted, leaving solid salt behind.

</Answer>

</Question>
