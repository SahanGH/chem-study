<Question number={3} topic="3.13">

These parts use the Beer–Lambert law:

$$
A = \varepsilon b c
$$

Here $c$ is the concentration, not the speed of light.

<Part label="a">

A solution has $A = 0.480$ in a 1.00 cm cell with $\varepsilon = 240\ \text{M}^{-1}\,\text{cm}^{-1}$. Find $c$.

<Answer>

0.00200 M

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

If the same solution is measured in a 2.00 cm cell, what is $A$?

<Answer>

0.960

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

Why does absorbance have no units?

<Answer>

In $A = \varepsilon b c$ the units cancel. Multiply the units of $\varepsilon$, $b$ and $c$:

$$
\text{M}^{-1}\,\text{cm}^{-1} \times \text{cm} \times \text{M}
$$

Nothing is left. Absorbance comes from comparing two amounts of light, the light sent into the sample and the light that comes out, so it is a pure number.

</Answer>

</Part>

</Question>

<Question number={4} title="A copper(II) calibration curve" topic="3.13" points={10}>

A student determines the concentration of a copper(II) solution by visible spectroscopy. Standards are measured at 635 nm in a 1.00 cm cell:

| $c$ (M) | $A$ |
|---|---|
| 0.0100 | 0.125 |
| 0.0200 | 0.250 |
| 0.0300 | 0.375 |
| 0.0400 | 0.500 |

<Part label="a">

On paper, plot $A$ versus $c$ and draw the best-fit line.

<Answer>

The four points, from (0.0100 M, 0.125) to (0.0400 M, 0.500), with $c$ on the horizontal axis and $A$ on the vertical, lie on one straight line. The best-fit line passes through all four and through the origin.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

Determine the slope of the line, including units.

<Answer>

$$
\frac{0.500}{0.0400} = 12.5\ \text{M}^{-1}
$$

This equals $\varepsilon b$.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

Calculate $\varepsilon$, stating its units.

<Answer>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon &= \frac{\text{slope}}{b} \\
&= \frac{12.5}{1.00} \\
&= 12.5\ \text{M}^{-1}\,\text{cm}^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="d">

An unknown gives $A = 0.300$. Determine its concentration.

<Answer>

$$
\begin{aligned}
c &= \frac{0.300}{12.5} \\
&= 0.0240\ \text{M}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="e">

The student dilutes the unknown to half its concentration and measures it again. Predict the new absorbance and justify your prediction.

<Answer>

$$
A = 0.150
$$

Absorbance is directly proportional to concentration at fixed $\lambda$ and $b$.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="f">

Explain why the calibration must be performed at a single fixed wavelength.

<Answer>

$\varepsilon$ is wavelength-dependent, so changing $\lambda$ changes the slope and the calibration no longer applies.

</Answer>

</Part>
