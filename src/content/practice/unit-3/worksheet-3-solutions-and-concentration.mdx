<Part label="c">

Explain in one sentence why $M_1V_1 = M_2V_2$ works.

<Answer>

Adding solvent changes the volume but not the number of moles of solute, so the product $MV$ (which equals moles) is conserved.

<Question number={4} topic="3.8">

On paper, draw a particulate representation of 0.10 M $\ce{MgBr2}$. Use filled circles for $\ce{Mg^2+}$ and open circles for $\ce{Br-}$, and show at least 3 magnesium ions. Omit the water molecules, and note on the drawing that you have done so.

<Answer>

At least 3 filled and 6 open circles ($1{:}2$ ratio), all separate and randomly distributed. They are never drawn as bonded $\ce{MgBr2}$ units. Caption: "water omitted for clarity."

</Answer>

</Question>

<Question number={5} topic="3.8">

A student draws 0.10 M $\ce{KCl}$ as eight intact "$\ce{KCl}$" pairs floating in water. Identify the error and its consequence.

<Answer>

$\ce{KCl}$ is ionic, so when it dissolves, its ions separate completely. The drawing must show 8 separate $\ce{K+}$ ions and 8 separate $\ce{Cl-}$ ions.

Drawn as neutral pairs, the solution would have no free ions and would not conduct electricity, but a solution of $\ce{KCl}$ does conduct (topic 3.2). The drawing also shows 8 particles where there are 16.

</Answer>

</Question>

<Question number={6} topic="3.7">

Describe how to prepare 500. mL of 0.200 M $\ce{CuSO4}$ from the solid, including the glassware.

<Answer>

$$
\begin{aligned}
n &= 0.200\ \text{mol/L} \times 0.500\ \text{L} \\
&= 0.100\ \text{mol}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= 0.100\ \text{mol} \\
&\quad \times 159.61\ \text{g/mol} \\
&= 16.0\ \text{g}
\end{aligned}
$$

Weigh 16.0 g of $\ce{CuSO4}$ on a balance. Dissolve it in roughly 300 mL of water in a beaker. Pour the solution into a 500-mL volumetric flask, and rinse the beaker into the flask with a little water so no copper sulfate is left behind. Then add water until the bottom of the meniscus sits on the line, stopper the flask and invert it to mix.

</Answer>

</Question>
