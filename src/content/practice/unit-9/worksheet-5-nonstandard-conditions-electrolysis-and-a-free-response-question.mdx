Work each question on paper before you open its answer. Nothing on this page needs the Nernst equation. Argue from $Q$ and $K$.

You will need these relationships.

Charge, in coulombs, from a current in amperes and a time in seconds:

$$
q = It
$$

Moles of electrons from charge:

$$
\text{moles of electrons} = \frac{q}{F}
$$

The standard cell potential:

$$
E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cathode}} - E^\circ_{\text{anode}}
$$

The standard free energy change, where $n$ is the moles of electrons in the balanced equation:

$$
\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ
$$

Use these values.

Standard reduction potentials:

| Half-reaction, written as a reduction | $E^\circ$ (V) |
|---|---|
| $\begin{aligned} &\ce{Ag+(aq) + e^- ->} \\ &\quad \ce{Ag(s)} \end{aligned}$ | $+0.80$ |
| $\begin{aligned} &\ce{Cu^2+(aq) + 2e^- ->} \\ &\quad \ce{Cu(s)} \end{aligned}$ | $+0.34$ |
| $\begin{aligned} &\ce{Zn^2+(aq) + 2e^- ->} \\ &\quad \ce{Zn(s)} \end{aligned}$ | $-0.76$ |

Molar masses:

| Element | Molar mass (g/mol) |
|---|---|
| $\ce{Ag}$ | 107.87 |
| $\ce{Cu}$ | 63.55 |
| $\ce{Al}$ | 26.98 |

Faraday's constant, $F$, is 96,485 C per mole of electrons.

The last question is the shape and length of a long free-response question on the AP exam. Write your whole answer to every part before you open an answer or the rubric. Then score yourself the way an exam reader would: no credit for anything that is not written down. The scoring rubric comes after the last part.

## Questions

<Question number={1} title="A cell's potential as it runs" topic="9.10">

A cell's voltage as it runs.

<Part label="a">

As a galvanic cell operates, $Q$ <Blank /> toward $K$ and $E_{\text{cell}}$ <Blank />.

<Answer>

$Q$: rises. $E_{\text{cell}}$: falls.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

The cell has run down when <Blank />, at which point $E_{\text{cell}} =$ <Blank />.

<Answer>

The cell has run down when: $Q = K$. $E_{\text{cell}}$ at that point: 0 V.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

Explain in one sentence what a cell potential physically measures.

<Answer>

How far the cell reaction currently sits from equilibrium: the farther from equilibrium, the larger the potential.

</Answer>

</Part>

</Question>

<Question number={2} title="Changing a concentration" topic="9.10">

For this reaction, predict the effect on $E_{\text{cell}}$ and justify each with $Q$. Do not calculate.

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\ce{Zn(s) + Cu^2+(aq) ->} \\
&\quad \ce{Zn^2+(aq) + Cu(s)}
\end{aligned}
$$

<Part label="a">

$[\ce{Cu^2+}]$ is increased. <Blank />

<Answer>

$E_{\text{cell}}$ increases: $Q$ falls.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

$[\ce{Zn^2+}]$ is increased. <Blank />

<Answer>

$E_{\text{cell}}$ decreases: $Q$ rises.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

More solid $\ce{Zn}$ is added. <Blank />

<Answer>

No change: solids are omitted from $Q$.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="d">

Write the reasoning behind part (a) in full, as a free-response answer needs.

<Answer>

$\ce{Cu^2+}$ appears in the denominator of $Q$, so raising its concentration lowers $Q$. A smaller $Q$ puts the cell farther from equilibrium, so $E_{\text{cell}}$ increases.

</Answer>

</Part>

</Question>

<Question number={3} title="A concentration cell" topic="9.10">

Concentration cells. Two $\ce{Cu}$ electrodes sit in solutions of 0.10 M and 1.0 M $\ce{Cu^2+}$.

<Part label="a">

$E^\circ_{\text{cell}}$ for this cell is <Blank />, because <Blank />.

<Answer>

$E^\circ_{\text{cell}}$: 0 V. Because: both half-cells have the same half-reaction, so the two standard reduction potentials are equal and cathode minus anode is zero.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

Yet it produces a voltage. Which half-cell is the anode, and why?

<Answer>

The dilute (0.10 M) half-cell. Copper dissolves there, raising its $\ce{Cu^2+}$ concentration, while $\ce{Cu^2+}$ plates out in the concentrated half-cell and lowers it. The cell runs in whichever direction moves the two concentrations toward each other.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

When does it stop?

<Answer>

When the concentrations are equal.

</Answer>

</Part>

</Question>

<Question number={4} title="The chain of Faraday's law" topic="9.11">

Faraday's law is a chain. Put the four conversions in order.

<Part label="a">

First: <Blank />

<Answer>

current and time to charge

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

Then: <Blank />

<Answer>

charge to moles of electrons

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

Then: <Blank />

<Answer>

moles of electrons to moles of substance

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="d">

Finally: <Blank />

<Answer>

moles to grams

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="e">

Which step needs the half-reaction, and why?

<Answer>

The third. The half-reaction gives the number of electrons per formula unit (one for $\ce{Ag+}$, two for $\ce{Cu^2+}$, three for $\ce{Al^3+}$), and that ratio cannot be guessed from the current.

</Answer>

</Part>

</Question>

<Question number={5} title="Plating silver" topic="9.11">

A current of 2.00 A flows for 30.0 min through a solution of $\ce{AgNO3}$. Calculate the mass of silver deposited.

<Answer>

The time in seconds:

$$
t = 1{,}800\ \text{s}
$$

The charge:

$$
\begin{aligned}
q &= (2.00)(1{,}800) \\
&= 3{,}600\ \text{C}
\end{aligned}
$$

The moles of electrons:

$$
\frac{3{,}600}{96{,}485} = 0.03731\ \text{mol}
$$

The half-reaction is one electron per atom, so the moles of silver equal the moles of electrons.

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\ce{Ag+(aq) + e^- ->} \\
&\quad \ce{Ag(s)}
\end{aligned}
$$

The mass of silver:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= (0.03731)(107.87) \\
&= \mathbf{4.02}\ \text{g}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

</Question>

<Question number={6} title="Plating copper" topic="9.11">

A current of 1.50 A is passed through a solution of $\ce{CuSO4}$ for 1.00 h.

<Part label="a">

Calculate the mass of copper deposited.

<Answer>

The charge:

$$
\begin{aligned}
q &= (1.50)(3{,}600) \\
&= 5{,}400\ \text{C}
\end{aligned}
$$

The moles of electrons:

$$
\frac{5{,}400}{96{,}485} = 0.0560\ \text{mol}
$$

The half-reaction needs two electrons per atom, so the moles of copper are half the moles of electrons.

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\ce{Cu^2+(aq) + 2e^- ->} \\
&\quad \ce{Cu(s)}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\frac{0.0560}{2} = 0.0280\ \text{mol Cu}
$$

The mass of copper:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= (0.0280)(63.55) \\
&= \mathbf{1.78}\ \text{g}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

A student reports 3.56 g. Identify the single omitted step.

<Answer>

The student did not divide the moles of electrons by 2. Each $\ce{Cu^2+}$ ion takes two electrons, so 0.0560 mol of electrons deposits 0.0280 mol of copper, not 0.0560 mol.

</Answer>

</Part>

</Question>

<Question number={7} title="How long for 5.00 g of copper" topic="9.11">

How long, in hours, does it take to deposit 5.00 g of copper at 2.00 A?

<Answer>

The moles of copper:

$$
\frac{5.00}{63.55} = 0.07868\ \text{mol Cu}
$$

The moles of electrons, two for each copper atom:

$$
2(0.07868) = 0.15736\ \text{mol}
$$

The charge:

$$
\begin{aligned}
q &= (0.15736)(96{,}485) \\
&= 15{,}183\ \text{C}
\end{aligned}
$$

The time in seconds, and then in hours (there are 3,600 s in an hour):

$$
\begin{aligned}
t &= \frac{15{,}183}{2.00} \\
&= 7{,}591.5\ \text{s} \\
&= \mathbf{2.11}\ \text{h}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

</Question>

<Question number={8} title="Aluminum and electricity" topic="9.11">

Producing aluminum requires three electrons per atom and $\ce{Al^3+}$/$\ce{Al}$ sits at $-1.66\ \text{V}$. Explain why aluminum smelters are built beside power stations, and why recycling saves so much energy.

<Answer>

Each mole of aluminum, only 26.98 g, takes three moles of electrons, and reducing $\ce{Al^3+}$ is strongly unfavored, so the supply must push hard. Both make the electrical energy for each kilogram very large. ($-1.66\ \text{V}$ is the value in water; a smelter works in a melt, because aluminum cannot be plated from water.) Recycling melts aluminum that is already reduced and skips the electrolysis.

</Answer>

</Question>

<Question number={9} title="A zinc and silver cell" topic="9.8, 9.9, 9.10" points={10}>

A galvanic cell is constructed from a zinc electrode in 1.0 M $\ce{Zn(NO3)2}$ and a silver electrode in 1.0 M $\ce{AgNO3}$, joined by a salt bridge.

<Part label="a">

Identify the anode and the cathode, and state the direction of electron flow through the external wire.

<Answer>

Zinc is the anode (oxidation, the lower reduction potential); silver is the cathode (reduction). Electrons flow through the wire from the zinc electrode to the silver electrode.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

Write the balanced overall cell reaction and give $n$.

<Answer>

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\ce{Zn(s) + 2Ag+(aq) ->} \\
&\quad \ce{Zn^2+(aq) + 2Ag(s)}
\end{aligned}
$$

$n = 2$

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

Calculate $E^\circ_{\text{cell}}$.

<Answer>

$$
\begin{aligned}
E^\circ_{\text{cell}} &= 0.80 - (-0.76) \\
&= \mathbf{+1.56}\ \text{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="d">

Calculate $\Delta G^\circ$ in kJ.

<Answer>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta G^\circ &= -nFE^\circ \\
&= -(2)(96{,}485)(1.56) \\
&= -301{,}033\ \text{J} \\
&= \mathbf{-301}\ \text{kJ}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="e">

The $\ce{AgNO3}$ solution is diluted to 0.10 M. Predict the effect on $E_{\text{cell}}$ and justify without calculating.

<Answer>

$\ce{Ag+}$ is a reactant, so lowering its concentration raises $Q$. A larger $Q$ puts the cell nearer equilibrium, so $E_{\text{cell}}$ decreases.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="f">

State the value of $E_{\text{cell}}$ when the cell has run down, and the relationship between $Q$ and $K$ at that moment.

<Answer>

$E_{\text{cell}} = 0\ \text{V}$ and $Q = K$.

</Answer>

</Part>
