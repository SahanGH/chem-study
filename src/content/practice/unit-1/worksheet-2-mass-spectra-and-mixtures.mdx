<Question number={1} topic="1.2">

Here is the mass spectrum of zirconium. The table gives each peak's mass, to the nearest amu, and its relative abundance.

| Mass (amu) | Relative abundance (%) |

<Part label="a">

How many naturally occurring isotopes does Zr have?

<Answer>

There are 5. The spectrum has five peaks, and each peak is one isotope.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

Without computing, is the average atomic mass closer to 90 amu or to 93 amu? Justify from the spectrum.

<Answer>

Closer to 90 amu. Picture the balance point. Over half the atoms (51.5%) are at 90 amu, and most of the rest are just above, at 91 and 92 amu (28.3% together). Only about a fifth (20.2%) are out at 94 or 96 amu. Measure from 91.5 amu, halfway between 90 and 93. The atoms at 94 and 96 amu are fewer than half as many as the atoms at 90 amu and, on average, less than twice as far away, so they pull less. The peaks at 91 and 92 amu sit half an amu to either side and nearly cancel. So the balance point is below 91.5 amu. The periodic table's value is 91.22 amu.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

Write, but do not evaluate, the full expression for the average atomic mass.

<Answer>

With the abundances as decimals, the average atomic mass $\bar m$ is:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar m &= 0.515(90) + 0.112(91) \\
&\quad + 0.171(92) + 0.174(94) \\
&\quad + 0.028(96)\ \text{amu}
\end{aligned}
$$

The masses here are rounded to whole numbers, the mass numbers, so this expression is an estimate. It comes to 91.3 amu, close to the periodic table's value.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="d">

Compare $\ce{^{90}Zr}$ and $\ce{^{94}Zr}$. What is identical for the two isotopes: <Blank /> What is different: <Blank />

<Answer>

Identical: the number of protons (40) and, in neutral atoms, the number of electrons (40). Their chemical behavior is almost identical too. Different: the number of neutrons (50 and 54), and so the mass.

</Answer>

</Part>

</Question>

<Question number={2} topic="1.2">

Antimony has two naturally occurring isotopes: $\ce{^{121}Sb}$ and $\ce{^{123}Sb}$. The periodic table lists Sb as 121.76 amu. Which isotope is more abundant, and *approximately* what fraction is it? Reason without long computation.

<Answer>

$\ce{^{121}Sb}$. The average, 121.76 amu, is below the midpoint, 122 amu, so it is nearer the lighter isotope, which must be the more abundant one. It sits 0.76 amu above 121 amu, on a gap of 2 amu. So the fraction of $\ce{^{123}Sb}$ is about

$$
\frac{0.76}{2} = 0.38
$$

or 38%, which leaves about 62% for $\ce{^{121}Sb}$. This is an estimate from the mass numbers.

Each isotope's mass is about 0.10 amu below its mass number (120.90 and 122.90 amu). So the average really sits 0.86 amu above the lighter isotope, and 0.86 ÷ 2.00 gives 43% for the heavier isotope and 57% for the lighter one. Those match the measured abundances.

</Answer>

</Question>

<Question number={3} topic="1.4">

On paper, draw a particulate diagram of a mixture of the diatomic element $X_2$ (filled circles) and the compound $XY$ (filled–open pairs). Include at least 3 particles of each.

<Answer>

Any drawing with at least 3 filled–filled pairs (the $X_2$ molecules) and at least 3 filled–open pairs (the $XY$ molecules), and nothing else: no single atoms and no open–open pairs. The two circles of a pair touch. All the filled circles are the same size, because they are the same element, $X$. Each pair is apart from the other pairs: they are separate molecules, not bonded into one lattice. Two kinds of particle are two piles, so the drawing shows a mixture.

</Answer>

</Question>

<Question number={4} topic="1.4">

A sample contains only $\ce{H2O}$ molecules. A student claims it must be a mixture because it contains two different elements. Evaluate the claim.

<Answer>

The claim is wrong. Every particle is the same kind, an $\ce{H2O}$ molecule, so the sample is a pure compound. "Mixture" requires more than one kind of *particle*, not more than one element.

</Answer>

</Question>

<Question number={5} topic="1.2" beyond>

Chlorine gas is made of $\ce{Cl2}$ molecules built from $\ce{^{35}Cl}$ (75.8%) and $\ce{^{37}Cl}$ (24.2%). In a mass spectrometer, whole $\ce{Cl2}$ molecules that have each lost one electron (molecular ions) give peaks at 70, 72 and 74 amu. Explain where the 72 amu peak comes from.

<Answer>

A $\ce{^{35}Cl-^{37}Cl}$ molecule:

$$
35\ \text{amu} + 37\ \text{amu} = 72\ \text{amu}
$$

The three peaks are the three isotope pairings: 35–35, 35–37 and 37–37.

The abundances set the heights: about 57% of the molecules are 35–35, 37% are 35–37 and 6% are 37–37. The mixed pairing counts twice, because either atom can be the heavy one.

</Answer>

</Question>
