<Question number={2} topic="1.5">

Each of these is written for a neutral atom. Classify each configuration as a ground state, an excited state, or impossible, and name the element where one exists.

<Part label="a">

1s² 2s² 2p²: <Blank />

<Answer>

Ground state of carbon: 2 + 2 + 2 = 6 electrons, no subshell is over its limit, and no subshell is left with room while a later one holds electrons.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

1s² 2s² 2p⁷: <Blank />

<Answer>

Impossible: a p subshell holds at most 6 electrons.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

1s² 2s² 2p⁵ 3s¹: <Blank />

<Answer>

An excited state of neon: 2 + 2 + 5 + 1 = 10 electrons and nothing is overfilled, but 2p holds 5 of its 6 electrons while 3s, which fills after it, holds 1.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Question number={3} topic="1.5">

How many *unpaired* electrons does a ground-state nitrogen atom have? An unpaired electron is one that is alone in its orbital. A sketch of three boxes, one for each of the three 2p orbitals, can support your count.

<Answer>

There are 3 unpaired electrons.

Nitrogen is 1s² 2s² 2p³. The 1s and 2s subshells are one orbital each, and each is full, so those four electrons are in pairs. The electrons of one subshell spread out: each takes an empty orbital of that subshell before any two share one (Hund's rule). The 2p subshell has three orbitals and holds three electrons, so each of the three is alone in its orbital.

A sketch shows one electron in each of the three boxes, and no box with two.

</Answer>

</Question>

<Part label="a">

Assign every peak to a subshell.

<Answer>

The peak at the highest binding energy is 1s, which holds 2 electrons, so heights of 1, 1, 3 and 1 stand for 2, 2, 6 and 2 electrons.

<Part label="b">

Identify the element, citing the total electron count as evidence.

<Answer>

$$
2 + 2 + 6 + 2 = 12\ \text{electrons}
$$

The element is magnesium. A neutral atom with 12 electrons has 12 protons.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

Why does the 2p peak sit at a *lower* binding energy than the 2s peak, even though both are in shell 2?

<Answer>

An s electron spends more of its time close to the nucleus than a p electron of the same shell, inside the shielding of the other electrons, so it feels more of the nuclear charge. The 2p electrons are shielded slightly more than the 2s electrons and are held less tightly: 5.31 against 9.07 MJ/mol.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="d">

Predict two specific changes in this spectrum for the element with one more proton.

<Answer>

The element is aluminum. All four peaks shift to a higher binding energy: aluminum's nucleus has 13 protons to magnesium's 12, and the electrons are in the same shells with the same electrons inside them. A new peak appears at the right, at a lower binding energy than the 3s peak. It is the 3p¹ electron, so it is half as tall as the 3s peak (1 electron).

</Answer>

</Part>

</Question>

<Question number={5} topic="1.6">

The 1s binding energy of carbon is 28.6 MJ/mol, and that of oxygen is 52.6 MJ/mol. Explain the difference in one sentence that uses Coulomb's law.

<Answer>

The 1s electrons of both atoms are in the first shell, with no electrons inside them to shield the nucleus, so by Coulomb's law oxygen's 8 protons attract them more strongly than carbon's 6 do, and more energy is needed to remove one, 52.6 against 28.6 MJ/mol.

</Answer>

</Question>
