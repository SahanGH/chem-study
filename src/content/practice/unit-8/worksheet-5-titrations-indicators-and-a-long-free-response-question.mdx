Work each question on paper before you open its answer. One habit runs through the titration questions: say what the flask holds before you choose a method.

This site writes H₃O⁺. The exam's equation sheet writes H⁺ for the same ion, and either is accepted.

Every solution here is at 25 °C unless a question says otherwise, so

$$
K_w = 1.0 \times 10^{-14}
$$

Use these values. The first acid in the table is acetic acid, $\ce{HC2H3O2}$ (also written $\ce{CH3COOH}$). Whenever you leave $x$ out, do the check and write its percentage in your answer.

| Substance | Constant | p$K_a$ |
|---|---|---|
| $\ce{HC2H3O2}$ | $K_a = 1.8 \times 10^{-5}$ | 4.74 |
| $\ce{HOCl}$ | $K_a = 3.5 \times 10^{-8}$ | 7.46 |
| $\ce{NH3}$ | $K_b = 1.8 \times 10^{-5}$ | |
| $\ce{NH4+}$ | $K_a = 5.6 \times 10^{-10}$ | 9.25 |

The indicators for this page:

| Indicator | Changes over pH |
|---|---|
| Methyl red | 4.4 to 6.2 |
| Bromothymol blue | 6.0 to 7.6 |
| Phenolphthalein | 8.2 to 10.0 |

A question gives the table of indicators it wants you to choose from, and you choose from that table. This one is not the table of topic 8.7's lesson.

The last question is written like one of the AP exam's long free-response questions and has a scoring rubric. Write your whole answer to every part before you open an answer or the rubric. Then score yourself the way an exam reader would: no credit for anything that is not written down. The scoring rubric comes after the last part.

## Questions

<Question number={1} title="Four stretches of one curve" topic="8.5">

For a weak acid titrated with a strong base, say what the flask holds and how the pH is found in each stretch.

<Part label="a">

Before any base is added.

<Answer>

$\ce{HA}$ only: an ICE table with $K_a$.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

Between the start and equivalence.

<Answer>

$\ce{HA}$ and $\ce{A-}$, a buffer: the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

At equivalence.

<Answer>

$\ce{A-}$ only: an ICE table with $K_b$.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="d">

After equivalence.

<Answer>

$\ce{A-}$ and leftover $\ce{OH-}$: the leftover $\ce{OH-}$ sets the pH, as a strong base alone would.

</Answer>

</Part>

</Question>

<Question number={2} title="A strong acid with a strong base" topic="8.4, 8.5">

25.0 mL of 0.100 M $\ce{HCl}$ is titrated with 0.100 M $\ce{NaOH}$.

<Part label="a">

Volume at equivalence: <Blank />

<Answer>

25.0 mL

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

pH before any base: <Blank />

<Answer>

1.00

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

pH after 10.0 mL.

<Answer>

$\ce{H3O+}$ left:

$$
2.50 - 1.00 = 1.50\ \text{mmol}
$$

It is in 35.0 mL.

$$
\begin{aligned}
[\ce{H3O+}] &= \frac{1.50}{35.0} \\
&= 0.0429\ \text{M}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\text{pH} = \mathbf{1.37}
$$

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="d">

pH at equivalence, with a reason.

<Answer>

7.00 at 25 °C: only $\ce{Na+}$ and $\ce{Cl-}$ remain, and neither reacts with water.

</Answer>

</Part>

</Question>

<Question number={3} title="Acetic acid with sodium hydroxide" topic="8.5">

50.0 mL of 0.10 M $\ce{HC2H3O2}$ is titrated with 0.10 M $\ce{NaOH}$.

<Part label="a">

Volume at equivalence: <Blank />

<Answer>

50.0 mL

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

pH before any base: <Blank />

<Answer>

The flask holds the weak acid alone, and $x$ is $[\ce{H3O+}]$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \sqrt{(1.8 \times 10^{-5})(0.10)} \\
&= \sqrt{1.8 \times 10^{-6}} \\
&= 1.34 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{M}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\text{pH} = \mathbf{2.87}
$$

The check:

$$
\frac{1.34 \times 10^{-3}}{0.10} \times 100\% = 1.3\%
$$

Under 5 percent, so the shortcut holds.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

pH after 25.0 mL, and what is special about it.

<Answer>

The half-equivalence point: $[\ce{HA}] = [\ce{A^-}]$, so the log term is zero and pH = p$K_a$ = 4.74.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="d">

pH at equivalence.

<Answer>

The flask holds acetate alone, in millimoles over milliliters:

$$
\begin{aligned}
[\ce{C2H3O2^-}] &= \frac{5.0}{100.0} \\
&= 0.050\ \text{M}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_b &= \frac{1.0 \times 10^{-14}}{1.8 \times 10^{-5}} \\
&= 5.6 \times 10^{-10}
\end{aligned}
$$

Acetate is a base, so $x$ is $[\ce{OH-}]$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \sqrt{(5.6 \times 10^{-10})(0.050)} \\
&= 5.3 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{M}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{pOH} &= -\log(5.3 \times 10^{-6}) \\
&= 5.28 \\
\text{pH} &= 14.00 - 5.28 \\
&= \mathbf{8.72}
\end{aligned}
$$

The check:

$$
\frac{5.3 \times 10^{-6}}{0.050} \times 100\% = 0.011\%
$$

Under 5 percent, so the shortcut holds.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="e">

Why is this equivalence point basic rather than neutral?

<Answer>

All the acid has been converted to acetate, the conjugate base of a weak acid, which takes protons from water and makes $\ce{OH-}$.

</Answer>

</Part>

</Question>

<Question number={4} title="Two curves compared" topic="8.5">

Compare the two titrations in questions 2 and 3, which use the same concentrations of acid and of base.

<Part label="a">

Are the equivalence *volumes* the same? Explain.

<Answer>

No: 25.0 mL and 50.0 mL. The difference has nothing to do with one acid being weak. Each flask needs as many moles of $\ce{OH-}$ as it holds of acid, and question 3's flask holds twice as many. In each titration the acid and the base have the same concentration, so the volume of base equals the volume of acid. Acid strength plays no part.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

Are the equivalence *pH values* the same? Explain.

<Answer>

No: 7.00 for $\ce{HCl}$ and 8.72 for acetic acid. The acetic acid titration leaves acetate, a weak base that reacts with water; the $\ce{HCl}$ titration leaves only $\ce{Na+}$ and $\ce{Cl-}$, both spectators.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

Which curve has a flat buffer region, and why?

<Answer>

The acetic acid curve. Between the start and equivalence both $\ce{HC2H3O2}$ and $\ce{C2H3O2^-}$ are present, so added base only changes their ratio, and pH depends on the log of that ratio.

</Answer>

</Part>

</Question>

<Question number={5} title="Ammonia with hydrochloric acid" topic="8.5">

100.0 mL of 0.050 M $\ce{NH3}$ is titrated with 0.10 M $\ce{HCl}$.

<Part label="a">

Volume at equivalence: <Blank />

<Answer>

50.0 mL

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

pH at the half-equivalence point, with a one-line reason.

<Answer>

At 25.0 mL, $[\ce{NH3}] = [\ce{NH4+}]$, so pH = p$K_a$ of $\ce{NH4+}$ = 9.25.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

pH at equivalence.

<Answer>

The flask holds $\ce{NH4+}$, in millimoles over milliliters:

$$
\begin{aligned}
[\ce{NH4+}] &= \frac{5.0}{150.0} \\
&= 0.0333\ \text{M}
\end{aligned}
$$

It is a weak acid, so $x$ is $[\ce{H3O+}]$. Use the $K_a$ of $\ce{NH4+}$ from the table at the top of this page.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \sqrt{(5.6 \times 10^{-10})(0.0333)} \\
&= 4.32 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{M}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\text{pH} = \mathbf{5.36}
$$

The check:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{4.32 \times 10^{-6}}{0.0333} \times 100\% \\
&\quad = 0.013\%
\end{aligned}
$$

Under 5 percent, so the shortcut holds.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="d">

Why is this equivalence point acidic?

<Answer>

Only $\ce{NH4+}$, a weak acid, is left (with $\ce{Cl-}$, a spectator).

</Answer>

</Part>

</Question>

<Question number={6} title="Indicators" topic="8.7">

Indicators.

<Part label="a">

The first blank: a weak acid. The second blank: color.

</Answer>

<Part label="b">

It changes color over roughly: <Blank />

Distinguish endpoint from equivalence point.

<Answer>

The equivalence point is fixed by reaction stoichiometry; the endpoint is where the indicator visibly changes. A well-chosen indicator makes them nearly coincide.

</Answer>

<Answer>

Phenolphthalein: its range, 8.2 to 10.0, holds 8.72.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="e">

Which suits question 5 (equivalence 5.36)?

<Answer>

Methyl red: its range, 4.4 to 6.2, holds 5.36.

</Answer>

<Question number={7} title="pH and solubility" topic="8.11">

pH and solubility, **in words only**.

<Part label="a">

State the test that decides whether a salt's solubility depends on pH.

<Answer>

Ask whether the anion is a meaningful base, or equivalently, whether its conjugate acid is weak. Only then does added $\ce{H3O+}$ take the anion out of solution.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

More soluble in acid? $\ce{CaCO3}$

<Answer>

Yes: the conjugate acid of its anion, $\ce{CO3^2-}$, is $\ce{HCO3-}$, a weak acid.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

More soluble in acid? $\ce{AgCl}$

<Answer>

No: the conjugate acid of its anion, $\ce{Cl-}$, is $\ce{HCl}$, a strong acid.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="d">

More soluble in acid? $\ce{Mg(OH)2}$

<Answer>

Yes: the conjugate acid of its anion, $\ce{OH-}$, is $\ce{H2O}$, a very weak acid.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="e">

Refute the claim "acids are reactive, so every salt dissolves better in acid."

<Answer>

$\ce{AgCl}$ is the counterexample: its solubility in 1 M $\ce{HNO3}$ is essentially the same as that in pure water, because $\ce{Cl-}$ is the conjugate base of a strong acid and essentially never takes a proton. What matters is how strong a base the anion is, not how strong the acid is.

</Answer>

</Part>

</Question>

<Question number={8} title="Hypochlorous acid titrated" topic="8.3, 8.4, 8.5, 8.7, 8.9" points={10}>

A 25.00 mL sample of 0.100 M hypochlorous acid, $\ce{HOCl}$, is titrated with 0.100 M $\ce{NaOH}$.

The $K_a$ of $\ce{HOCl}$ is $3.5 \times 10^{-8}$ (p$K_a$ 7.46), and $K_w = 1.0 \times 10^{-14}$. Three indicators are on hand:

| Indicator | Changes over pH |
|---|---|
| Methyl red | 4.4 to 6.2 |
| Bromothymol blue | 6.0 to 7.6 |
| Phenolphthalein | 8.2 to 10.0 |

<Part label="a">

Calculate the volume of $\ce{NaOH}$ needed to reach equivalence.

<Answer>

25.00 mL

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

Calculate the pH before any base is added.

<Answer>

The flask holds the weak acid alone, and $x$ is $[\ce{H3O+}]$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \sqrt{(3.5 \times 10^{-8})(0.100)} \\
&= 5.9 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{M}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\text{pH} = \mathbf{4.23}
$$

The check:

$$
\frac{5.9 \times 10^{-5}}{0.100} \times 100\% = 0.059\%
$$

Under 5 percent, so the shortcut holds.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

Calculate the pH after 12.50 mL of base, and explain why it could have been written down without calculation.

<Answer>

This is half the equivalence volume, so half the $\ce{HOCl}$ has become $\ce{OCl-}$ and $[\ce{HOCl}] = [\ce{OCl^-}]$. The log term is zero and pH = p$K_a$ = 7.46.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="d">

Calculate the pH after 20.00 mL of base.

<Answer>

After the base has reacted, in millimoles:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\ce{HOCl} &= 2.500 - 2.000 \\
&= 0.500\ \text{mmol} \\
\ce{OCl-} &= 2.000\ \text{mmol}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{pH} &= 7.46 + \log\frac{2.000}{0.500} \\
&= 7.46 + 0.60 \\
&= \mathbf{8.06}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="e">

Calculate the pH at the equivalence point.

<Answer>

All 2.500 mmol is $\ce{OCl-}$ in 50.00 mL, so

$$
[\ce{OCl^-}] = 0.0500\ \text{M}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_b &= \frac{1.0 \times 10^{-14}}{3.5 \times 10^{-8}} \\
&= 2.9 \times 10^{-7}
\end{aligned}
$$

$\ce{OCl-}$ is a base, so $x$ is $[\ce{OH-}]$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \sqrt{(2.9 \times 10^{-7})(0.0500)} \\
&= 1.2 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{M}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{pOH} &= -\log(1.2 \times 10^{-4}) \\
&= 3.92 \\
\text{pH} &= 14.00 - 3.92 \\
&= \mathbf{10.08}
\end{aligned}
$$

The check:

$$
\frac{1.2 \times 10^{-4}}{0.0500} \times 100\% = 0.24\%
$$

Under 5 percent, so the shortcut holds.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="f">

Select an indicator and justify.

<Answer>

Phenolphthalein. Of the three, its range (8.2 to 10.0) lies nearest the equivalence pH of 10.08, which is just above its top, so its color change is complete just before the equivalence point. Methyl red and bromothymol blue would both have changed completely while a good deal of the acid was still left. Even phenolphthalein is not ideal here: it starts to change at pH 8.2, when about 21 mL of the 25.00 mL have gone in, so the titration has to run to the full color. An indicator whose range held 10.08 would be better.

</Answer>

</Part>
