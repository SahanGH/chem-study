Work each question on paper before you open its answer. One idea runs through the worksheet: neutral means $[\ce{H3O+}] = [\ce{OH-}]$, not pH 7.

Topic 4.8 taught conjugate acid–base pairs, and questions 1 to 4 use them.

This site writes H₃O⁺. The exam's equation sheet writes H⁺ for the same ion, and either is accepted.

Every solution here is at 25 °C unless a question says otherwise, with $K_w = 1.0 \times 10^{-14}$. Question 6 is the one at another temperature.

You will need these relationships. The second, and the value of $K_w$ in the third, are for 25 °C. The third holds for a conjugate pair.

$$
\text{pH} = -\log[\ce{H3O+}]
$$

$$
\text{pH} + \text{pOH} = 14.00
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_a \times K_b &= K_w \\
&= 1.0 \times 10^{-14}
\end{aligned}
$$

Label all four species in this equilibrium.

$$
\begin{aligned}

<Answer>

<Question number={3} title="Water as a base" topic="8.1">

Explain why water can act as a Brønsted–Lowry base, referring to its structure.

<Answer>

Its oxygen carries two unshared electron pairs, either of which can form a covalent bond to a proton, which is exactly what accepting a proton means.

</Answer>

</Question>

<Part label="e">

Rank as bases, weakest first: $\ce{Cl-}$, $\ce{F-}$, $\ce{CN-}$. For $\ce{HF}$, $K_a = 7.2 \times 10^{-4}$; for $\ce{HCN}$, $K_a = 6.2 \times 10^{-10}$.

<Answer>

$\ce{Cl-} < \ce{F-} < \ce{CN-}$. $\ce{Cl-}$ is the conjugate base of a strong acid, so it is so weak a base that it essentially never takes a proton from water; among the rest, base strength runs opposite to conjugate acid strength, and $\ce{HF}$ is a much stronger acid than $\ce{HCN}$.

</Answer>

<Question number={6} title="Neutral water at 50 °C" topic="8.1">

Explain why pure water at 50 °C has a pH below 7 yet is still neutral.

<Answer>

$K_w$ increases with temperature, so both $[\ce{H3O+}]$ and $[\ce{OH-}]$ rise and the pH falls below 7. The solution stays neutral because neutrality is defined by $[\ce{H3O+}] = [\ce{OH-}]$, not by the number 7, which applies only at 25 °C.

</Answer>

</Question>

<Question number={8} title="Find the error" topic="8.2">

A student calculates the pH of 0.010 M $\ce{Sr(OH)2}$ as 12.00. Identify the error and give the correct value.

<Answer>

They used $[\ce{OH-}] = 0.010$ M, but strontium hydroxide releases two hydroxide ions per formula unit, so $[\ce{OH-}] = 0.020$ M. Then pOH = 1.70 and pH = 12.30.

</Answer>

</Question>

<Question number={9} title="The six strong acids" topic="8.2, 8.6">

Name the six strong acids, and say which common acid is conspicuously absent from the list.

<Answer>

$\ce{HCl}$, $\ce{HBr}$, $\ce{HI}$, $\ce{HNO3}$, $\ce{H2SO4}$ (its first proton), $\ce{HClO4}$. $\ce{HF}$ is absent: despite fluorine's electronegativity it is a weak acid, mainly because the H–F bond is exceptionally strong.

</Answer>

</Question>
