## Questions

<Question number={1} topic="4.7">

Assign the oxidation number of the named element.

<Part label="a">

Mn in $\ce{MnO2}$: <Blank />

<Answer>

Oxygen is $-2$, and the numbers of a neutral compound add up to zero:

$$
x + 2(-2) = 0
$$

So manganese is $+4$.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

Cr in $\ce{Cr2O3}$: <Blank />

<Answer>

Oxygen is $-2$, and the numbers of a neutral compound add up to zero:

$$
2x + 3(-2) = 0
$$

So each chromium is $+3$.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

S in $\ce{SO3^2-}$: <Blank />

<Answer>

Oxygen is $-2$, and the numbers add up to the ion's charge, not to zero:

$$
x + 3(-2) = -2
$$

So sulfur is $+4$.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="d">

N in $\ce{NH4+}$: <Blank />

<Answer>

Hydrogen is $+1$, because it is bonded to a nonmetal, and the numbers add up to the ion's charge:

$$
x + 4(+1) = +1
$$

So nitrogen is $-3$.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="e">

Cl in $\ce{ClO2-}$: <Blank />

<Answer>

Oxygen is $-2$, and the numbers add up to the ion's charge:

$$
x + 2(-2) = -1
$$

So chlorine is $+3$.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="f">

O in $\ce{H2O2}$: <Blank />

<Answer>

Hydrogen is $+1$, because it is bonded to a nonmetal. The rule for hydrogen comes before the rule for oxygen, so hydrogen is settled first, and the sum then gives the oxygen:

$$
2(+1) + 2x = 0
$$

So each oxygen is $-1$. Hydrogen peroxide is a peroxide, and this is the number oxygen has in a peroxide.

</Answer>

</Part>

</Question>

<Question number={2} topic="4.7">

For each reaction in parts (a) to (c): is it an oxidation–reduction (redox) reaction? If so, name what is oxidized and what is reduced.

<Part label="a">

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\ce{Cu(s) + 2 AgNO3(aq) ->} \\
&\quad \ce{Cu(NO3)2(aq) + 2 Ag(s)}
\end{aligned}
$$

<Answer>

Redox. Copper goes from 0 to $+2$, so the copper metal is oxidized. Silver goes from $+1$ to 0, so the silver ion is reduced. Silver nitrate, $\ce{AgNO3}$, is the reactant that holds that ion, so "silver nitrate is reduced" is also a correct answer.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\ce{BaCl2(aq) + Na2SO4(aq) ->} \\
&\quad \ce{BaSO4(s) + 2 NaCl(aq)}
\end{aligned}
$$

<Answer>

Not redox. No oxidation number changes. This is a precipitation.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\ce{CH4(g) + 2 O2(g) ->} \\
&\quad \ce{CO2(g) + 2 H2O(g)}
\end{aligned}
$$

<Answer>

Redox. Carbon goes from $-4$ to $+4$, so methane, $\ce{CH4}$, is oxidized. Oxygen goes from 0 to $-2$, so oxygen gas, $\ce{O2}$, is reduced.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="d" beyond>

The species that is reduced is called the oxidizing agent, and the species that is oxidized is called the reducing agent. Name both agents in each reaction above that is redox.

<Answer>

In (a), the silver ion is reduced, so it is the oxidizing agent. As the equation is written, that is silver nitrate. Copper is oxidized, so copper metal is the reducing agent.

In (c), oxygen gas is reduced, so it is the oxidizing agent. Methane is oxidized, so it is the reducing agent.

Reaction (b) is not redox, so it has neither.

</Answer>

</Part>

</Question>

<Question number={3} topic="4.7" beyond>

The species that is reduced is called the oxidizing agent, and the species that is oxidized is called the reducing agent.

A student writes: "Zinc is oxidized, so zinc is the oxidizing agent." Correct the statement, and explain why the species that is oxidized is given the name "reducing agent".

<Answer>

Zinc is the *reducing* agent. The label names what the species does to its partner: by giving up electrons, zinc causes the other species to be reduced. The species oxidized is always the reducing agent.

</Answer>

</Question>

<Question number={4} topic="4.5">

Solution stoichiometry: find each quantity.

<Part label="a">

Moles of $\ce{Ba^2+}$ in 35.0 mL of 0.200 M $\ce{BaCl2}$: <Blank />

<Answer>

Into moles, with the volume in liters. Each formula unit of barium chloride supplies one barium ion, so the moles of barium ions are the moles of barium chloride.

$$
\begin{aligned}
&0.200\ \text{mol/L} \\
&\quad \times 0.0350\ \text{L} \\
&= 7.00 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

Mass of $\ce{BaSO4}$ ($M = 233.39\ \text{g/mol}$) if all of that barium precipitates: <Blank />

<Answer>

Across by the ratio. Each barium ion ends up in one formula unit of barium sulfate:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\ce{Ba^2+(aq) + SO4^2-(aq) ->} \\
&\quad \ce{BaSO4(s)}
\end{aligned}
$$

So the moles of barium sulfate are the moles of barium ions. Out of moles, through the molar mass:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&7.00 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol} \\
&\quad \times 233.39\ \text{g/mol} \\
&= 1.63\ \text{g}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

Volume of 0.150 M $\ce{Na2SO4}$ needed to react exactly with the barium ions of part (a): <Blank />

<Answer>

The equation in part (b) uses one sulfate ion for every barium ion, and each formula unit of sodium sulfate supplies one sulfate ion. So the moles of sodium sulfate needed are the moles of barium ions. Out of moles, by dividing by the molarity:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= \frac{7.00 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}}{0.150\ \text{mol/L}} \\
&= 0.0467\ \text{L} \\
&= 46.7\ \text{mL}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

</Part>

</Question>

<Question number={5} topic="4.5">

40.0 mL of 0.100 M $\ce{AgNO3}$ is mixed with 40.0 mL of 0.150 M $\ce{NaCl}$. Silver chloride is insoluble.

<Part label="a">

Moles of each reactant: <Blank />

<Answer>

Into moles, with each volume in liters. Each formula unit of silver nitrate supplies one silver ion, and each formula unit of sodium chloride supplies one chloride ion.

$$
\begin{aligned}
&0.100\ \text{mol/L} \\
&\quad \times 0.0400\ \text{L} \\
&= 4.00 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}\ \ce{Ag+}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
&0.150\ \text{mol/L} \\
&\quad \times 0.0400\ \text{L} \\
&= 6.00 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}\ \ce{Cl-}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

Find the limiting reactant, with reasoning.

<Answer>

The two ions join one to one:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\ce{Ag^+(aq) + Cl^-(aq) ->} \\
&\quad \ce{AgCl(s)}
\end{aligned}
$$

Suppose all the silver ions react. They use an equal number of moles of chloride ions, $4.00 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}$. There is $6.00 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}$ of chloride ions, more than enough. So $\ce{Ag+}$, from the silver nitrate, is the limiting reactant, and the chloride ion is in excess.

With a 1 : 1 ratio, the reactant with fewer moles is the one that runs out. With any other ratio, fewer moles does not settle it: set the moles against the coefficients, as above.

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="c">

Mass of $\ce{AgCl}$ formed ($M = 143.32\ \text{g/mol}$): <Blank />

<Answer>

The product comes from the limiting reactant. Each silver ion ends up in one formula unit of silver chloride, so the moles of silver chloride are the moles of silver ions.

$$
\begin{aligned}
&4.00 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol} \\
&\quad \times 143.32\ \text{g/mol} \\
&= 0.573\ \text{g}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="d">

Moles of excess reactant remaining: <Blank />

<Answer>

Subtract what the limiting reactant used from what was there.

$$
\begin{aligned}
&6.00 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol} \\
&\quad - 4.00 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol} \\
&= 2.00 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}\ \ce{Cl-}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

</Part>

</Question>

<Question number={6} topic="4.6">

Titration calculations.

<Part label="a">

25.0 mL of $\ce{HCl}$ needs 28.6 mL of 0.125 M $\ce{NaOH}$ to reach the equivalence point. Find $[\ce{HCl}]$.

<Answer>

**The reaction.** The acid and the base react one to one:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\ce{HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) ->} \\
&\quad \ce{NaCl(aq) + H2O(l)}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Titrant delivered.** Molarity times volume, with the volume in liters.

$$
\begin{aligned}
&0.125\ \text{mol/L} \\
&\quad \times 0.0286\ \text{L} \\
&= 3.575 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol NaOH}
\end{aligned}
$$

**From titrant to analyte.** At the equivalence point the base added has exactly used up the acid. The ratio is one to one, so the sample held the same number of moles of hydrochloric acid.

**Back to the sample.** Divide by the volume of the sample, in liters.

$$
\begin{aligned}
[\ce{HCl}] &= \frac{3.575 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}}{0.0250\ \text{L}} \\
&= 0.143\ \text{M}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

</Part>

<Part label="b">

20.0 mL of 0.200 M $\ce{H2SO4}$ is titrated with 0.250 M $\ce{KOH}$. Sulfuric acid gives up both of its protons to the base. What volume of base is needed to reach the equivalence point?

<Answer>

**The reaction.** Sulfuric acid is an acid with two protons to give, so one mole of it takes two moles of potassium hydroxide:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\ce{H2SO4(aq) + 2 KOH(aq) ->} \\
&\quad \ce{K2SO4(aq) + 2 H2O(l)}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Acid in the flask.** Molarity times volume, with the volume in liters.

$$
\begin{aligned}
&0.200\ \text{mol/L} \\
&\quad \times 0.0200\ \text{L} \\
&= 4.00 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}\ \ce{H2SO4}
\end{aligned}
$$

**From acid to base.** Each formula unit of potassium hydroxide supplies one hydroxide ion, and each $\ce{H2SO4}$ takes two of them.

$$
\begin{aligned}
&2 \times 4.00 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol} \\
&= 8.00 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}\ \ce{OH-}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Volume of base.** Out of moles, by dividing by the molarity:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= \frac{8.00 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}}{0.250\ \text{mol/L}} \\
&= 0.0320\ \text{L} \\
&= 32.0\ \text{mL}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

</Part>

</Question>
