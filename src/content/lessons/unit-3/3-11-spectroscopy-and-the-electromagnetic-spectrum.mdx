An electric heater with glowing coils can warm your face for an hour and never sunburn it. The midday summer sun can burn the same skin in well under an hour. Most of the heater's light is infrared, which you feel but cannot see. The burn comes from the sun's ultraviolet light. Both are light, but they do different things to the molecules in your skin. The difference is how much energy each packet of light carries.

## A spectrum of light

The light you can see is one small band of something much wider. **Electromagnetic radiation** is energy that travels through space as a wave. Visible light is one kind. Infrared, ultraviolet and microwaves are others.

Picture ripples crossing a pond. The **wavelength** of a wave is the distance from one crest to the next.

Light is also delivered in packets. A **photon** is one packet of light energy. Topic 3.12 says how much energy each photon carries. For now you need one fact, in words: the shorter the wavelength, the more energy each photon carries.

The exam uses four regions of the spectrum. Here they are, from the longest wavelengths to the shortest:

| Region | Typical wavelengths |
|---|---|
| Microwave | about 1 m to 1 mm |
| Infrared | about 1 mm to 700 nm |
| Visible | about 700 nm (red) to 400 nm (violet) |
| Ultraviolet | about 400 to 10 nm |

The borders are conventions, not sharp lines. Radio waves lie beyond the microwaves, at longer wavelengths still. X-rays lie beyond the ultraviolet, at shorter ones.

A wavelength may be given in millimeters, micrometers or nanometers:

- 1 mm = 10⁻³ m
- 1 µm = 10⁻⁶ m
- 1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m

Each step down the table is a step to shorter wavelengths, so it is also a step up in energy per photon. A microwave photon carries the least energy of the four, and an ultraviolet photon the most. The same rule holds inside the visible band. Its violet end, near 400 nm, carries more energy per photon than its red end, near 700 nm.

## Energy comes in steps

A molecule can take in energy in three ways that matter here. It can **rotate**, which means spin as a whole. It can **vibrate**, which means its bonds stretch and bend. And its electrons can move to higher energy levels.

Each kind of energy comes only in certain amounts, like the rungs of a ladder. A molecule can be on a rung, never between two. The rungs are its energy levels.

A single atom is simpler than a molecule. It has no bonds to vibrate and nothing to spin, so it has only electronic levels.

A molecule absorbs a photon only when the photon's energy matches the gap between the rung it is on and a higher one. The photon disappears, and its energy goes into the molecule.

It works the other way too. A molecule that goes down a rung can give out a photon, and that photon carries exactly the energy of the gap. That is emission.

The three ladders are spaced very differently. Rotational levels are closest together. Vibrational levels are farther apart. Electronic levels are farthest apart.

<KeyIdea>

The size of the step decides the region. A small step needs a low-energy, long-wavelength photon. A big step needs a high-energy, short-wavelength photon.

</KeyIdea>

## Three regions, three motions

Each kind of step has its own region of the spectrum.

**Microwaves change rotation.** A gas molecule that absorbs a microwave photon spins faster.

A microwave oven works in this region of the spectrum, but it is a cousin of this process, not an example of it. In liquid water the molecules cannot spin freely. The oven's waves twist the polar water molecules back and forth, and the jostling spreads out as heat.

**Infrared changes vibration.** A molecule that absorbs an infrared photon vibrates more: a bond stretches and squeezes, or bends, with more energy. That is why infrared warms things. Vibrating molecules jostle their neighbors.

**Ultraviolet and visible light move electrons.** A photon of ultraviolet or visible light moves an electron to a higher energy level. Chemists call that jump an **electronic transition**. This is where color comes from: a colored substance absorbs some visible wavelengths and lets the rest through or reflects it. It is also why ultraviolet light can damage skin. A molecule with an electron moved up can react in ways it otherwise would not.

Each match works in reverse for emission. A molecule dropping to a lower level gives out a photon in the same region.

<Worked title="Three absorptions of carbon monoxide">

Carbon monoxide gas, CO, absorbs light at 2.60 mm, at 4.67 µm and at 154 nm. All three are real, measured wavelengths. Which kind of change does each cause, and how do the energy steps compare?

1. **Put each wavelength in meters and find its region.**

   - 2.60 mm = 2.60 × 10⁻³ m. That is between 1 m and 1 mm: microwave.
   - 4.67 µm = 4.67 × 10⁻⁶ m, which is 4670 nm. That is between 1 mm and 700 nm: infrared.
   - 154 nm = 1.54 × 10⁻⁷ m. That is between 400 nm and 10 nm: ultraviolet.

2. **Match each region to its change.** Microwave: the molecule spins faster, a rotational step. Infrared: the bond between C and O vibrates more, a vibrational step. Ultraviolet: an electron moves to a higher level, an electronic step.

3. **Compare the steps.** Energy per photon is inversely proportional to wavelength (topic 3.12 gives the equation). So a ratio of two wavelengths is the inverse ratio of the two energies. Microwave to infrared:

   $$
   \frac{2.60 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{m}}{4.67 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{m}} = 557
   $$

   Infrared to ultraviolet:

   $$
   \frac{4.67 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{m}}{1.54 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{m}} = 30.3
   $$

   The vibrational step is about 560 times the rotational step, and the electronic step is about 30 times the vibrational step. That makes the electronic step about 17,000 times the rotational step:

   $$
   \frac{2.60 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{m}}{1.54 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{m}} = 1.69 \times 10^{4}
   $$

4. **Check against the ladder.** Smallest step for rotation, middle for vibration, largest for electrons: the same order as the regions.

Radio telescopes pick up 2.60 mm light given out by carbon monoxide in the clouds between the stars, as its molecules drop back to slower spinning.

</Worked>

<Trap>

Four mistakes come up again and again on this topic:

- **Thinking a longer wavelength means more energy.** It is the other way round: the shorter the wavelength, the more energy per photon. Microwaves carry the least energy per photon of the four regions.
- **Matching infrared to electrons.** Infrared changes vibrations. Moving an electron to a higher level takes ultraviolet or visible light.
- **Thinking any photon with "enough" energy is absorbed.** For a jump between two levels in the molecule, the photon's energy must match the gap exactly. A photon with more than the gap is not absorbed for that jump. Knocking an electron right out of an atom, as in photoelectron spectroscopy (topic 1.6), is a different process.
- **Putting visible light at an end of the order.** It sits between infrared and ultraviolet, and its violet end carries more energy than its red end.

</Trap>

<KeyPoints>

- In order of increasing energy per photon, the regions are microwave, infrared, visible and ultraviolet, because a shorter wavelength means more energy per photon.
- A molecule's rotational, vibrational and electronic energies come in steps, and it absorbs or emits a photon only when the photon's energy matches a step.
- Microwave photons change rotation, infrared photons change vibration, and ultraviolet and visible photons move electrons between energy levels.
- Rotational steps are smallest, vibrational steps larger and electronic steps largest.
- The region in which a substance absorbs tells you which kind of change happened.

</KeyPoints>
