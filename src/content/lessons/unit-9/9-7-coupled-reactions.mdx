Most of the copper in the wiring of a house was, at one stage of its making, a dark gray solid: copper(I) sulfide, Cu₂S. Sealed in a vessel and left to itself, copper(I) sulfide stays copper(I) sulfide. In a smelter, air is blown through it, and copper metal collects at the bottom of the furnace. This lesson is about what the air does.

## Unfavored, on its own

Here is the change the smelter wants:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\ce{Cu2S(s) -> 2Cu(s) + S(s)} \\
&\Delta G^\circ = +86.2\ \text{kJ/mol}_\text{rxn}
\end{aligned}
$$

The value is for 25 °C, and it is positive: the change is thermodynamically unfavored (topic 9.3). All three substances are pure solids, so there is no $K$ to write (topic 9.5), and the sign says it all. It is the reverse change that is favored. Copper and sulfur combine, and copper(I) sulfide does not come apart.

There are two ways to get the copper anyway. One brings in energy from outside. The other brings in a second reaction.

## Energy from outside

**Electricity.** A current from a power supply can push a reaction in its unfavored direction. An electrolytic cell is a device that uses a current to drive an unfavored reaction, and topic 9.8 builds one. Charging a battery is the same thing: the charger runs the battery's own favored reaction backward.

**Light.** A green leaf makes glucose and oxygen from carbon dioxide and water, a change that is unfavored by a wide margin. It goes only while light falls on the leaf. This is photosynthesis.

What do these have in common? The energy comes from a source that is not one of the reacting substances, and the change goes only while the source is on. The reaction's ΔG° is not altered. It is still unfavored, which is why a charged battery can give the energy back.

## A favored partner

Back to the furnace. The oxygen of the air reacts with sulfur in a strongly favored reaction. At 25 °C:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\ce{S(s) + O2(g) -> SO2(g)} \\
&\Delta G^\circ = -300.1\ \text{kJ/mol}_\text{rxn}
\end{aligned}
$$

You have added equations twice before. Topic 6.9 added equations and added their ΔH° values. Topic 7.6 added equations and multiplied their $K$ values. Free energy behaves like enthalpy: **when equations are added, their ΔG° values are added.** (These are one rule: ΔG° is proportional to $\ln K$, and adding logarithms is multiplying the numbers.)

The equation sheet has no line for this rule, so it is yours to remember.

<Worked title="Copper, with air">

Find ΔG° at 25 °C for the reaction that takes place when air meets copper(I) sulfide, and say whether it is thermodynamically favored.

1. **Write the two equations with their values.**

   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   &\ce{Cu2S(s) -> 2Cu(s) + S(s)} \\
   &\Delta G^\circ = +86.2\ \text{kJ/mol}_\text{rxn}
   \end{aligned}
   $$

   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   &\ce{S(s) + O2(g) -> SO2(g)} \\
   &\Delta G^\circ = -300.1\ \text{kJ/mol}_\text{rxn}
   \end{aligned}
   $$

2. **Find what they share.** Sulfur is a product of the first and a reactant of the second, one mole in each.

3. **Add the equations.** The sulfur cancels.

   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   &\ce{Cu2S(s) + O2(g) ->} \\
   &\quad \ce{2Cu(s) + SO2(g)}
   \end{aligned}
   $$

   Check the atoms: 2 Cu, 1 S and 2 O on each side.

4. **Add the values, signs included.**

   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   \Delta G^\circ &= +86.2 + (-300.1) \\
   &= -213.9\ \text{kJ/mol}_\text{rxn}
   \end{aligned}
   $$

5. **Read it.** The sum is below zero: the overall reaction is thermodynamically favored. Unlike the first reaction, it has gases in it, so it has a $K_p$, and topic 9.5 gives its size. Each 5.7 kJ/mol is a factor of ten:

   $$
   \frac{213.9}{5.7} = 37.5
   $$

   That is 37.5 factors of ten, so $K_p$ is between 10³⁷ and 10³⁸. At equilibrium the gas over the solids would be sulfur dioxide with almost no oxygen left in it.

</Worked>

**What "share" means.** In the furnace no heap of sulfur forms and then burns. The sulfur atoms pass from copper to oxygen, and the two changes happen as one reaction. That is what **coupling** means: an unfavored reaction and a favored one joined through a substance that one of them makes and the other uses, so that they go as a single overall reaction.

**Why the shared substance is needed.** Burning a pile of sulfur in the next room would do nothing for the copper(I) sulfide, however favored the burning is. Two reactions with no substance in common are two separate reactions, and adding their ΔG° values is arithmetic with no chemistry behind it. Only when the sulfur that leaves the copper is the same sulfur that the oxygen takes does the sum describe something that happens.

**Nothing is cheated.** The overall reaction, the one that really takes place, has ΔG° below zero. The unfavored step did not become favored: the total did.

These values are for 25 °C. The smelter runs its furnace hot because at 25 °C the favored overall reaction would be far too slow (topic 9.4), and the overall reaction is favored at both temperatures.

<KeyIdea>

An unfavored reaction can be driven by a favored one when two things are true: the two share a substance that one makes and the other uses, and their ΔG° values add up to less than zero.

</KeyIdea>

## Coupling in a living cell

Cells have to build molecules whose formation is unfavored, at one temperature, with no furnace. They do it by coupling, and the favored partner is nearly always the same one: the reaction of **ATP** (adenosine triphosphate) with water, which gives ADP (adenosine diphosphate) and a phosphate ion. Its ΔG° is −30.5 kJ/mol.

ATP is not "energy stored in a bond". Breaking any bond takes energy in (topic 6.7). What counts is ATP's whole reaction with water, the bonds broken and the bonds made together. Its ΔG° of −30.5 kJ/mol is large enough to bring the sum below zero for any reaction that is unfavored by less than 30.5 kJ/mol, as long as the two can be made to share a substance.

A cell holds very little ATP, and a sprinting muscle would use all of its own within a few seconds. So the ADP has to be turned back into ATP all the time, and that reaction is the reverse of the one above. Reversing an equation changes the sign of its ΔG°, as it does for ΔH° (topic 6.9): making ATP from ADP and phosphate has ΔG° = +30.5 kJ/mol. It is unfavored, and it needs a favored partner of its own.

<Worked title="ATP for a sprint">

In the first seconds of a sprint, muscle remakes its ATP with the help of a compound it keeps in store, phosphocreatine. In words, the two reactions are these.

ADP + phosphate → ATP + H₂O, with ΔG° = +30.5 kJ/mol

phosphocreatine + H₂O → creatine + phosphate, with ΔG° = −43.0 kJ/mol

Both values are for a neutral solution at 25 °C, the conditions biochemists' tables use. Show that the two can be coupled, and find what they share.

1. **Find what they share.** Phosphate is made by the second reaction and used by the first. (Water goes the other way: made by the first, used by the second.)

2. **Add the equations.** Phosphate and water cancel:

   phosphocreatine + ADP → creatine + ATP

3. **Add the values, signs included.**

   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   \Delta G^\circ &= +30.5 + (-43.0) \\
   &= -12.5\ \text{kJ/mol}
   \end{aligned}
   $$

   Below zero: the overall reaction is thermodynamically favored.

4. **Look at how the cell does it.** If the two reactions simply ran side by side in the same water, the phosphocreatine would react with water, its phosphate would drift away, and the ADP would stay ADP. An enzyme (topic 5.11) holds phosphocreatine and ADP together, and the phosphate group passes straight from one to the other. No free phosphate ion appears along the way. The phosphate group is what the two reactions share.

5. **Read it.** Use topic 9.5's terms, with 5.7 kJ/mol for each factor of ten. Alone, making ATP is unfavored by 30.5 kJ/mol:

   $$
   \frac{30.5}{5.7} = 5.35
   $$

   That is 5.35 factors of ten below 1, a $K$ of about 4.5 × 10⁻⁶. Coupled, it is favored by 12.5 kJ/mol:

   $$
   \frac{12.5}{5.7} = 2.2
   $$

   That is 2.2 factors of ten above 1, a $K$ of about 160.

</Worked>

The ATP made this way is spent the way it was made. It does not react with water in the open, where its favored reaction would drive nothing. An enzyme joins it to the reaction it is to drive, through something the two share.

## The smelter of topic 4.5

Two reactions that share a substance have passed one of the two tests. The other is the sum, and it has to be worked out each time. Topic 4.5 left a pair to try.

<Trap>

Eight mistakes cost points on this topic:

- **Adding two ΔG° values for reactions that share nothing.** Without a substance that one makes and the other uses, the sum describes no reaction.
- **Stopping at "the favored one is bigger".** A full answer gives the sum, with its sign, and names the shared substance.
- **Calling a pair coupled when the sum is still positive.** In the aluminum smelter, sharing oxygen was not enough.
- **Subtracting the values, or dropping a sign,** when the equations are added. The values are added, signs included.
- **Saying the unfavored reaction "became favored".** Its own ΔG° is unchanged. The overall reaction is the favored one.
- **"ATP stores energy in a bond that releases it when broken."** Breaking a bond takes energy in. It is ATP's whole reaction with water that is favored.
- **Confusing a coupled reaction with a catalyst.** The partner is used up and changes the overall ΔG°. A catalyst is not used up and does not change it.
- **Forgetting that favored is not fast.** The coupled reactions of industry are run hot for speed.

</Trap>

<KeyPoints>

- An unfavored reaction ($\Delta G^\circ > 0$) does not go far toward its products on its own: with a large positive ΔG° it makes at most a trace. There are two ways to make it happen: energy from outside, or a coupled reaction.
- The outside sources are electricity (an electrolytic cell, the charging of a battery) and light (photosynthesis). The change goes only while the source is on, and the reaction's ΔG° is not altered.
- When equations are added, their ΔG° values are added, as their ΔH° values are, while their $K$ values are multiplied.
- Two reactions are coupled when they share a substance that one makes and the other uses. The coupling drives the unfavored one only if the sum of the two ΔG° values is below zero.
- In living cells the favored partner is usually ATP reacting with water (ΔG° = −30.5 kJ/mol), and the ATP hands a phosphate group to the molecule being changed. ATP is itself remade by coupling.
- When coupling drives a reaction, the overall reaction has $\Delta G^\circ < 0$, so nothing is cheated. Where no partner makes the sum negative, as in the aluminum smelter, the energy must come from outside.

</KeyPoints>
